Melanoma Overview

Melanoma is the most serious type of skin cancer. It develops in the melanocytes, the cells that produce melanin, the pigment that gives skin its color.

Key Characteristics:
- Can develop anywhere on the body, but most commonly on areas exposed to the sun
- More common in people with fair skin, but can occur in all skin types
- Often resembles a mole, or may develop from an existing mole
- Early detection is critical for successful treatment

Warning Signs (ABCDE Rule):
A - Asymmetry: One half of the mole doesn't match the other half
B - Border: Irregular, scalloped, or poorly defined edges
C - Color: Varied colors within the same mole (brown, black, tan, red, white, or blue)
D - Diameter: Larger than 6mm (about the size of a pencil eraser), though melanomas can be smaller
E - Evolving: Changes in size, shape, color, elevation, or development of new symptoms like bleeding or itching

Risk Factors:
- Excessive UV exposure from sun or tanning beds
- History of sunburns, especially blistering sunburns in childhood
- Fair skin, light hair, and light eyes
- Family history of melanoma
- Large number of moles (more than 50)
- Weakened immune system

Types of Melanoma:
1. Superficial spreading melanoma: Most common type, grows along the top layer of skin
2. Nodular melanoma: Second most common, grows deeper into skin more quickly
3. Lentigo maligna melanoma: Typically occurs in older adults, often on sun-damaged skin
4. Acral lentiginous melanoma: Rare type found on palms, soles, or under nails

Diagnosis:
- Skin examination by dermatologist
- Dermoscopy for detailed mole analysis
- Skin biopsy for definitive diagnosis
- Staging tests if melanoma is confirmed

Treatment depends on stage but may include:
- Surgical excision (removal of the melanoma and surrounding tissue)
- Lymph node biopsy or removal
- Immunotherapy for advanced melanomas
- Targeted therapy for specific genetic mutations
- Radiation therapy in certain cases

Prevention:
- Avoid peak sun hours (10 AM to 4 PM)
- Use broad-spectrum sunscreen (SPF 30+)
- Wear protective clothing and wide-brimmed hats
- Avoid tanning beds
- Regular self-examinations
- Annual skin checks by dermatologist for high-risk individuals

Prognosis:
Early-stage melanoma has excellent survival rates when detected and treated promptly. The 5-year survival rate for localized melanoma is over 99%. However, survival rates decrease significantly if melanoma spreads to lymph nodes or distant organs.
